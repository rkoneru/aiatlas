LLM as a judge

A capable model, given a clear rubric, grades the outputs of another model, agent or person: scoring answers, comparing two versions, checking against a reference, or ticking off a checklist. It makes evaluation fast and cheap enough to run on every release and every conversation. But a judge is itself a model that needs checking: calibrate it against human labels and design out its known biases.

Checklist for a trustworthy judge

  

    
1 · Define "good" first
Write the rubric with anchored examples for each score before you automate anything. Vague criteria give noisy judges.

    
2 · Label a human set
Have experts grade 50–200 examples. This is the ground truth the judge is measured against.

    
3 · Measure agreement
Compare judge and human verdicts (agreement, Cohen's κ, precision on failures). Iterate the prompt until it's good enough for the decision it drives.

    
4 · Prefer binary and pairwise
Pass/fail per criterion and A-vs-B comparisons are more stable than 1–10 scores.

    
5 · Use a different model
Avoid grading a model with itself; use a different family or a panel of judges for high-stakes calls.

    
6 · Keep humans sampling
Re-check a sample every release. Judges drift when the product, the data or the judge model changes.
