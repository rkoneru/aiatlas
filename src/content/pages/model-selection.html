Model selection & strategy

A foundation model is trained once on broad data and adapted to thousands of tasks. Your job is to pick the right tier for each task, work around its knowledge cutoff, and choose the cheapest adaptation that works: prompt, retrieve, fine-tune or distill. Foundation models

What makes a model "foundational"

Self-supervised pre-training on web-scale data gives general capabilities (language, code, reasoning, vision) that transfer to tasks it was never explicitly trained on. Post-training then shapes it into an assistant. One model serves chat, extraction, coding and agents.

Knowledge cutoff

The model knows nothing after its training data ends, and it may not know exactly when that was. It will confidently describe "current" prices, laws or leaders from the past. Fixes: put today's date in the system prompt, give it web search or retrieval for anything time-sensitive, and instruct it to say when information may be stale.

Knowledge distillation

Teacher → student

A large teacher model generates outputs (or full probability distributions) on your task; a small student is trained to reproduce them.

When it pays off

High, steady volume on a well-defined task where a frontier model works but is too slow or expensive. Typical result: most of the quality at a fraction of the cost.

Pitfalls

The student inherits the teacher's errors, and narrow students fail outside their task. Check license terms: some providers restrict using outputs to train competing models.

Build vs buy vs partner

Buy

SaaS or platform

Fastest to value for common problems (support bots, meeting notes, coding assistants). Risk: lock-in, generic workflows, data residency.

Choose when the use case is not a differentiator.

Partner

Platform + your logic

Build on an agent platform or a vendor's APIs with your own prompts, tools, data and evals. The common middle path for enterprises.

Choose when you need customization without running infrastructure.

Build

Own the stack

Custom orchestration, self-hosted or fine-tuned models, your own eval and observability. Maximum control and differentiation, maximum ownership cost.

Choose when AI is the product or the core advantage.

Avoiding lock-in

Abstract the model call. A gateway or thin client lets you swap providers per route.

Own your evals. A portable eval suite is what makes switching safe.

Use open protocols. MCP for tools and A2A for agents keep integrations portable.

Scaling, emergence & the frontier

Scaling laws

Loss falls predictably as parameters, data and compute grow. Compute-optimal training (the "Chinchilla" result) uses roughly 20 training tokens per parameter; many modern models train far beyond that to make inference cheaper.

Emergent abilities

Some skills look like they appear suddenly at a certain scale. Researchers debate how real the jumps are: with smoother, continuous metrics, many abilities improve gradually, and the "jump" is partly an artifact of all-or-nothing scoring.

AGI & frontier outlook

Frontier labs say they are pursuing general-purpose systems that match or exceed humans on most cognitive work. Timelines are genuinely contested, from a few years to many decades. Definitions differ too. For planning, assume capabilities keep improving quickly and design systems that benefit from better models (thin harnesses, strong evals).

What this means for builders

Re-run your evals on each new model release. Delete scaffolding the new model doesn't need. Keep humans on irreversible decisions regardless of benchmark scores. Budget for prices falling and capabilities rising.
