AI strategy, roadmap & adoption

Most AI programs that stall don't fail on technology. They fail on focus (too many scattered pilots), foundations (data, platform, governance) or adoption (people don't change how they work). Walk from ambition to measured value.

Building the strategy

Ambition

Tie AI to business outcomes

Start from three to five measurable business goals (cost to serve, cycle time, revenue per rep, risk losses), not from "use AI". Decide where AI should differentiate you and where it is just table stakes.

Portfolio

Balance quick wins and big bets

Score candidate use cases on value, feasibility, risk and data readiness (see RICE). Fund a mix: productivity quick wins, process redesign, and a few differentiating products. Kill pilots that miss their metrics.

Foundations

Build once, reuse everywhere

A shared platform (model gateway, RAG, agent runtime, evals, observability), governed data access, and a governance process. Without these, every team rebuilds the same plumbing.

Operating model

Who does what

Common pattern: a central AI enablement team (platform, standards, governance) with embedded builders in business units. Forward-deployed engineers bridge the two by building with the business.

Roadmap horizons

HorizonFocusTypical outcomes

12+ monthsTransformRedesigned processes and roles, AI in products, continuous improvement loop, measurable P&L impact

Change management & adoption

Why people resist

Fear for jobs, distrust of outputs, extra work during transition, and tools that don't fit their real workflow.

What works

Visible executive use, champions in every team, role-specific training with real tasks, and redesigning the workflow rather than bolting AI on.

Trust

Be clear about what AI does and doesn't decide, keep humans accountable, show sources, and make it easy to report bad outputs.

Measure adoption

Weekly active users, tasks completed with AI, time saved (sampled, not self-reported only), quality and satisfaction.

Redesign roles

Move people from doing the work to reviewing, handling exceptions and improving the system. Plan reskilling paths early.

Communicate

Explain the why, share wins and failures openly, and update policies as tools and laws change. See AI compliance.
