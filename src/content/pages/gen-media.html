Generative media: diffusion, GANs & CLIP

Image, video and audio generators mostly work by learning to remove noise. Start from pure static, and a network guided by your prompt cleans it up step by step until a picture appears. Walk a text-to-image pipeline, then scrub through the noise schedule.

Noise schedule

Training adds noise to real images (left to right) and teaches the model to undo it. Generation runs the other way: from pure noise at step T back to a clean image at step 0.

The approaches

Diffusion

Learn to denoise

The forward process gradually adds Gaussian noise over T steps. A network (U-Net or diffusion transformer) learns to predict that noise. Sampling starts from static and subtracts predicted noise step by step. Latent diffusion does this in a compressed VAE space, which makes it fast enough for consumer GPUs and video.

GANs

Generator vs discriminator

A generator makes fakes; a discriminator tries to spot them; both improve until fakes are convincing. GANs produce sharp images in one pass but are unstable to train and prone to mode collapse. Diffusion has largely replaced them, though GAN ideas survive in fast distilled samplers and super-resolution.

CLIP · multimodal embeddings

One space for text and images

Train an image encoder and a text encoder so matching pairs land close together (contrastive learning on hundreds of millions of captioned images). Enables zero-shot image classification, text-to-image search, and conditioning signals for generators.

Text-to-image & video

Prompt to pixels

A text encoder turns the prompt into conditioning; the denoiser attends to it at every step. Classifier-free guidance sharpens prompt adherence. Video models add a time dimension (spatio-temporal patches). Control adds-ons (ControlNet, image prompts, inpainting masks) steer layout and style.

Video, world models & physical AI

Video generation

Text- and image-to-video models now produce short, high-resolution clips with synchronized audio and consistent characters. Enterprise uses include marketing variants, training content, product demos and localization. Likeness rights and disclosure matter most here.

World models

Models that generate interactive, explorable environments and predict how a scene changes when an agent acts. They're used to train and test robots and autonomous systems in simulation.

Physical AI

Vision-language-action models connect perception and language to robot control. See Robotics & embodied AI.

Labeling duties

Since 2 August 2026 the EU AI Act requires machine-readable marking of AI-generated content and disclosure of deepfakes. Use C2PA content credentials and keep generation logs.
