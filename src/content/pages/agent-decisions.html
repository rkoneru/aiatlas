Agent goals & decision making

Decision making turns reasoning into action: the agent generates options, scores them against its goals and constraints, picks one, acts, and learns from the result. Good agents also know when not to decide: when confidence is low or the stakes are high, the right decision is to ask a person.

Five kinds of decisions

Deterministic

Rules give one answer. "If battery < 10% → charge."

Probabilistic

Outcomes are uncertain; choose by likelihood.

Multi-criteria

Balance cost, time, risk, quality.

Sequential

Today's choice changes tomorrow's options.

Adaptive

The policy improves from feedback over time.

Decision lab

Options are scored on weighted criteria. Hard constraints veto an option outright. Each option's chance of succeeding turns the score into an expected value, and if the best option's confidence is below your threshold, the agent escalates to a person instead of acting.

Exploration vs exploitation

Deciding under uncertainty

Bayesian reasoning

Update beliefs as evidence arrives.

Monte Carlo simulation

Sample many futures and compare outcomes.

MDPs and POMDPs

Model sequential decisions, with full or partial observation of the state.

Reinforcement learning

Learn the policy from reward over many episodes.

Confidence thresholds

Act only above a confidence bar; otherwise ask or gather more information.

Risk-aware scoring

Penalize variance and worst cases, not only the average.

Agent goals

Goals tell an agent what it is trying to achieve. They steer what it pays attention to, how it plans, which action it picks and when it stops. No goal means no direction; a vague goal means wasted steps; a goal with no measure means the agent can't tell whether it succeeded.

Five types of goals

Task

Achieve a defined outcome.

"Book a flight to New York."

State

Reach or hold a condition.

"Keep the room at 20–24 °C."

Optimization

Maximize or minimize a metric.

"Minimize travel time and cost."

Learning

Improve knowledge or skill.

"Learn this user's preferences."

Alignment

Act safely, fairly and within values.

"Help users safely and fairly."

Choosing actions by goals

Agents score each option by how much it advances each active goal, weighted by priority. Change the weights or scores to see which option wins.

Goal management

Create & prioritize

Turn user intent into goals; rank by importance and urgency.

Update & retire

Revise goals as context changes; drop ones that no longer apply.

Track & resolve conflicts

Measure progress; resolve clashes by priority, weights or by asking the user.

Goal design checklist

Common failure modes

  • Ambiguous or unclear goals
  • Conflicting goals with no priority
  • Wrong success metric, so the agent optimizes the proxy
  • Short-term goals crowding out long-term ones
  • Over-optimizing one goal and ignoring others (including safety)

Tools & frameworks

  • Goal-Oriented Action Planning (GOAP)
  • Hierarchical Task Networks (HTN)
  • Behavior trees
  • Reward functions and reinforcement learning
  • Plan-and-execute agent patterns in LLM frameworks
