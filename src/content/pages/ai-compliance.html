Responsible AI, regulation & compliance

AI compliance is now a patchwork: comprehensive AI laws (EU, South Korea), targeted rules (China's labeling rules, US state laws on hiring, frontier models and chatbots), privacy law applied to AI (GDPR, CCPA), sector regulators (finance, health, employment) and voluntary standards (NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001). United States: federal vs state

Federal

Executive Order 14365 and preemption

Signed 11 December 2025, the order seeks a "minimally burdensome" national framework. It created a DOJ AI Litigation Task Force (operating from 10 January 2026) to challenge state AI laws, asked Commerce to list "onerous" state laws, and was followed by a March 2026 national policy framework that is a legislative roadmap rather than regulation. Child safety, data-center infrastructure and state procurement are carved out.

What it means: state laws stay enforceable until a court rules or Congress preempts them. Keep complying.

Existing federal law still applies

No AI exemption

FTC Act §5 covers deceptive AI claims and unfair practices. Title VII / ADA cover discriminatory hiring tools. ECOA and FCRA require specific adverse-action reasons even when a model decides. HIPAA covers health data sent to models. FDA regulates AI-enabled medical devices. Bank model risk guidance (SR 11-7) applies to AI models in finance.

Data privacy & AI

Lawful basis

Under GDPR, every use of personal data, including training and fine-tuning, needs a legal basis (consent, contract, legitimate interests…). Document it per purpose.

Impact assessments

A DPIA (GDPR) or risk assessment (CCPA regulations) is expected for high-risk processing such as profiling, sensitive data or large-scale monitoring.

Automated decisions

GDPR Art. 22 gives people rights around solely automated decisions with legal or similar effects: human review, explanation, contest. California's ADMT rules add notice, opt-out and access rights.

Minimization & retention

Send models only what the task needs. Set retention for prompts, outputs and logs. Redact PII before indexing. Delete on schedule.

Vendors & transfers

Data processing agreements, zero-retention or no-training terms, regional hosting, and a lawful mechanism for cross-border transfers.

Rights vs model weights

Deleting a person from a database is easy; removing them from trained weights is not. Keep personal data in retrieval stores (deletable) rather than in fine-tuned weights where possible.

Responsible AI & regulation

Responsible AI turns values into checks you can run: is it fair, can we explain it, is it honest rather than flattering, is it documented, is its output labeled, are we within the law, and are we managing frontier risks? The principles in practice

Bias & fairness

Equal treatment across groups

Measure outcomes per group: selection rates (demographic parity), error rates (equalized odds), true-positive rates (equal opportunity). The US "four-fifths rule" flags a selection ratio below 0.8. Fairness definitions can conflict, so pick the one that matches the harm you're preventing and document why.

Explainability

Why did it decide that?

For classic ML: feature importance, SHAP and LIME attributions, counterfactuals ("approved if income were $5k higher"). For LLMs: citations, reasoning summaries and tool traces. Mechanistic interpretability goes further, looking for the internal features and circuits that drive behavior.

Sycophancy

Agreeing instead of being right

Preference training can teach models to flatter users, cave to pushback or mirror their opinions. Test for it: ask the same question with and without a stated user opinion, and push back on correct answers. Mitigate with targeted training data, system-prompt instructions and evals.

Model & system cards

Documentation as a control

A model card covers intended use, training data summary, evaluations, limitations and safety testing. A system card covers the deployed product: tools, guardrails, human oversight. Both are increasingly expected by regulators and enterprise buyers.

Copyright, IP & licensing

Inputs, outputs and weights

Three questions: was training data used lawfully; who owns generated output (often not copyrightable without human authorship, depending on the country); and what does the model license allow? Many vendors offer IP indemnity for enterprise customers. Get legal review before shipping.

Watermarking & provenance

Label what's synthetic

C2PA content credentials attach signed provenance metadata to media. Invisible watermarks such as Google's SynthID embed a detectable signal in images, audio, video or text. Both can be stripped or degraded, so treat them as signals, not proof.

Frontier AI safety

Managing catastrophic risk

Frontier labs publish safety frameworks (for example, responsible scaling policies) that tie stronger safeguards to dangerous-capability evaluations in areas like cyber, bio and autonomy. Regulators increasingly require systemic-risk assessments for the largest general-purpose models.

Standards

NIST AI RMF & ISO/IEC 42001

NIST's AI Risk Management Framework (Govern, Map, Measure, Manage) is the common US reference. ISO/IEC 42001 is a certifiable AI management system standard. See the governance maturity model.
