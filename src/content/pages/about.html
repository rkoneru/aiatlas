About the AI Architecture Atlas

A free, interactive guide to how modern AI systems are designed, built, governed and paid for. It is written for four kinds of reader at once: executives deciding where AI fits, customers and business owners scoping a project, forward-deployed engineers delivering one, and anyone learning the field from scratch.

Who builds it

Created and maintained by Rakesh, Director of AI for Forward-Deployed Engineering, leading a team that delivers generative, agentic and voice AI for enterprises. Before that, he built and ran an automation practice across the US and India. He holds a Master's in Electrical Engineering and created the ABL and AGIL frameworks described in this Atlas.

Views are his own and don't represent any employer or vendor.

Why it exists

Most AI material is either a vendor pitch or a research paper. Teams need something in between: the patterns that actually ship, drawn as systems you can step through, with the numbers, risks and decisions that sit around them. Every page tries to answer three questions: how does it work, when should I use it, and what goes wrong.

How it stays honest

  • Sources are listed at the bottom of every page. Prefer the primary source: specs, standards, papers and official docs.
  • Time-sensitive pages (protocols, regulations, products) carry a "last reviewed" badge and are rechecked monthly; see What's changed.
  • No invented statistics. Figures come from a named source or from a calculator where you enter your own numbers.
  • Where a popular infographic gets something wrong, the page says so.

Vendor neutrality

The patterns are vendor-neutral: RAG, agents, MCP, A2A, evaluation and governance work the same on any model or platform. Product names appear as examples, not endorsements. The hands-on pages use Claude because the Atlas itself runs on it, so every "try it" button works in place; the same steps apply to other assistants.
