Layers of an AI voice agent

What's actually working under the call. A good voice agent is not just a voice. It is a connected business system. Trace one call through the layers, then explore each layer and budget your latency.

The six layers

Latency budget

Gaps in human conversation are short. If the agent's first audio arrives too late, callers talk over it or hang up. Tune each stage to see the time to first audio.

  • Stream everything: partial transcripts, streamed LLM tokens, streamed TTS.
  • Speak a short acknowledgement ("Let me check that…") while a slow tool runs.
  • Keep the model and voice servers in the caller's region, and cap tool timeouts with a fallback line.

Cascaded vs speech-to-speech

Cascaded (speech-to-text → LLM → text-to-speech)Speech-to-speech (native audio model)
How it worksThree models: transcribe, think in text, speakOne model hears audio and answers in audio
LatencySum of three stages plus network; streaming each stage keeps it workableLowest: replies can start within a few hundred milliseconds
NaturalnessTurn-taking needs separate voice-activity and end-of-turn logicBetter tone, interruptions (barge-in) and backchannels ("mm-hm")
Control & complianceText at every step: easy to log, redact, check and apply guardrailsTranscripts are a side output; harder to check before it speaks
Tools & logicMature function calling on text modelsSupported, with fewer model choices
VoicesMix best-in-class parts and voicesLimited to the model's own voices
Best forRegulated flows, complex tools, IVR replacementConversational, low-latency experiences

Full-duplex models listen while they speak, so callers can interrupt naturally. Whichever you choose, keep a text transcript for QA and compliance, tell callers they're talking to AI, and test with real line noise and accents. Many teams run a hybrid: speech-to-speech for the conversation, with tools and policy checks in text. See Contact-center AI architecture.
