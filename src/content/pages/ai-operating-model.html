AI operating model, roles & workforce

Strategy says what to do with AI; the operating model says who does it and how decisions get made. Three choices matter most: how centralized the AI team is, which roles you hire or grow, and how you bring the rest of the workforce along. Most AI programs that stall have the technology working and the organization not.

Pick an operating model

Centralized CoE

One AI team builds and runs everything for the business.

+ Consistent standards, scarce talent pooled, fast start
− Becomes a bottleneck; far from the business

Fits: early maturity, small or highly regulated organizations

Hub-and-spoke

A central hub owns platform, governance and standards; teams in the business units build on it.

+ Scale with guardrails; business ownership
− Needs clear decision rights and funding rules

Fits: most mid-to-large organizations once past the first pilots

Federated

Each business unit runs its own AI teams; a small central group sets policy.

+ Speed and closeness to the business
− Duplicated platforms, uneven quality and risk

Fits: high maturity, very autonomous business units

Redesign work, task by task

AI changes tasks, not whole jobs. List the tasks in one role, estimate the hours and pick the kind of work; the tool suggests what to automate, what to augment and what to keep human.

Bring the workforce along

AI literacy, in four tiers

  • Everyone: what AI is and isn't, approved tools, data rules, how to check output
  • Power users: prompting, building personal workflows, spotting failure modes
  • Builders: RAG, agents, evaluation, security by design
  • Leaders: value cases, risk appetite, redesigning work, leading change

Adoption playbook

  1. Explain why, and what it means for each role, before the tools arrive
  2. Start with willing teams and visible quick wins
  3. Name champions in every team; give them time, not just titles
  4. Change processes and targets, not just tools
  5. Measure use and outcomes, not licences
  6. Consult employee representatives early where law or agreements require it
Say plainly what happens to time saved: more capacity for growth, better service, reskilling into new roles, or slower hiring. Silence on this is the fastest way to lose trust and adoption.
